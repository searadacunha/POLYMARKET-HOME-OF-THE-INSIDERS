## 1. Highest-priority NEW financial investigation: KPMG earnings, 19 core + 3 expanded

[Bitquery Research's September 13 cross-chain investigation](https://www.bitquery.io/investigations/polymarket-insider-trading-kpmg-clients) contradicts the interpretation that the 19 accounts have no on-chain relationships. It describes **11 amount- and time-matched withdrawal-to-funding handoffs** through a bridge and ordinary intermediary wallets. In the initial 19 accounts, 16 are linked by those 11 routes, another by a combined funding route, and the last two by overlapping payout destinations; searches of counterparties added **perlgrow**, **boosthero** and **bostonguy** to reach 22. These are third-party reconstructed financial relationships, not locally redetected receipts.

The report's **19-account core** has 144 positions in 99 markets. On **21 KPMG-audited earnings markets**, it recorded **19 winning markets and 2 losing**, versus **14.3 expected winners** from paid entry prices, a reported tail probability **p = 0.0084** (about 1/120) under Bitquery's market-level probability model. Those markets carried **$53,442 stakes / +$21,519 results**. On **15 non-KPMG earnings markets** it won 11 (11.3 expected); on 48 sport/crypto/oil markets it won 31 (29.4 expected), with a slightly negative profit. Comparing markets rather than every account's identical market bets avoids pseudoreplication.

**43 of 44 positions with an identifiable SEC filing date** were opened before results became public; **$53,173 of $53,420** matching positions was committed before filing. Bitquery reports that the single market with a late-first entry was a $3 CarMax trade; other small additions after filings totaled $247. The total 99-market population is not the same as the 21 KPMG markets, and a subset was unresolved. Numbers and p-values are **third-party results**, not independently replicated here; the cohort was selected after its suspicious performance was noticed, so p = 0.0084 is **not** a valid unconditional insider probability.

The cross-chain path to test first is **Youaregu's last withdrawal → bridge/intermediary → lawrence3's first deposit (2025-11-06, $78.51 received, 28 s)**, followed by **lawrence3 → showredto (2025-11-08, $598.29, 18 s)**. The original withdrawal tx is `0xafed93e173e88e6b1be9f4990bc95770c473c978cd8c0eeb064a58552872e696`, and destination transaction `0xaa6df4010b275200a3fe3617886b1d389d8db53afef1028ac7fabd1f73358d90`. The primary chain receipts have not been fetched/decoded here.

- [KPMG 22-account name registry](KPMG_WALLETS.csv) contains **19 core and 3 expanded names**. **Only four full proxy addresses** are linked by the cited article. All other proxy columns remain empty rather than being fabricated.
- [11 reported cross-chain handoffs](KPMG_HANDOFFS.csv) provides day, amounts, arrival lag and evidence status. None is promoted to `CONFIRMED_IN_THIS_REPO` until matched logIndex, contract, block and bridge record are archived.
- The bridge `0xf70da97812cb96acdf810712aa562db8dfa3dbef` serves millions of unrelated addresses and is *not* a controller; payouts to `0xabf9261806a71a46fe06a05b3a68820905cbdb9e` have more discriminatory potential but still need counterparty controls. One intermediary `0xedb181ff29b26e0069193ffa79dc62eb0f15e112` displays address-poisoning behavior; do not assign it to the network.
- A federal inquiry **reported about an employee of KPMG** is not shown to refer to these wallets. Do not equate any trader's pseudonym with that employee. No public criminal charge against that person is established by these sources.

**Why this is new:** the earlier [external-case registry's X1 row](../external-polymarket-cases-2026-11-07/CASES.csv) contains only a $21k / 19-account secondary pointer. This follow-up supplies a specific **inter-account funding graph**, raw transaction reference candidates, control markets and the 22-account extension. It does not rewrite the initial source artifact.

## 2. Midterm 2026 circular trading: a 544-wallet cohort, not an insider cohort

The [Anti-Corruption Data Collective's November 2 briefing](https://acdatacollective.org/work/with-election-day-approaching-prediction-market-election-integrity-risks-remain/) identifies **591 unusually low-return round trips**, involving **544 distinct wallets on 22 Polymarket U.S. congressional markets**; approximately **$618,395 gross throughput** with **$34 aggregate result**. The screen examined high-priced shares bought at about 95 cents or more, quickly sold back with very small profit or loss. Reconstruct exact source thresholds and exclusions before applying them to any local trade list. A round trip can reflect market-making, fee/reward optimization or independent traders rather than wash trading, which requires additional evidence about beneficial control or market intent.

The same dataset spans **28,845 midterm election markets on three platforms** (not 28,845 Polymarket markets). ACDC reported that 96% of **15,985 markets with live order books across those platforms** could be moved five cents for $3,500 or less, and that the top 1% of Polymarket wallets accounted for 75% of Polymarket congressional volume. These percentages refer to **different measurement universes**, and are not wallet-level insider probabilities. There is no supplied 544-wallet CSV or full transaction list here. Preserve as a quantitative benchmark and targeted data retrieval specification.

## 3. Ethiopia and Romania: post-outcome closed-loop volume

[WIRED, 2026-10-15](https://www.wired.com/story/over-dollar600-million-traded-on-polymarket-election-contracts-after-races-ended/) reported over $600 million combined volume in the Ethiopian and Romanian election markets after political outcomes were effectively decided or elections had been cancelled. For Ethiopia, MSR Decode reportedly attributed about **$284 million of $285 million** to repeated two-wallet transactions, with over **38,000 rapid loops** and more than **98% financially neutral** loops; median holding time about **9 seconds**. In the old Romanian market, researchers reported **up to 99.6% flagged volume during some specific weeks**, **not** 99.6% over the entire market history.

**Interpretation:** these are potential volume farming / circular-market-activity cases. The data currently support investigation, **not** claims that account owners were identical, that the activity was illegal, or that a hypothetical token airdrop was its purpose. Exact market IDs, the full maker/taker tape, positions and receipt hashes remain uncollected. Do not treat $284M of nominal trading volume as capital stolen or realized profit.

## 4. XFM paired wallets: independently documented complementary trading

[MSR Decode, updated 2026-11-01](https://msrdecode.com/cases/xfm-paired-wallets.html) identified **47 complete numbered account pairs** in a **selected 97-profile sample**; **40 pairs** entered the same markets in the same sequence. A separately analyzed **18-wallet subset** has **117 transactions** with corresponding CTF split/merge events and transfers to paired counterparties. Nine June 23 transactions produced exactly **1,779.93 YES and 1,779.93 NO** shares in matched pairs for the Anthropic ticker market; each pair's prices summed to one unit.

Our [nine-transaction register](XFM_NINE_SPLITS.csv) includes the **full Polygon tx hashes** linked by MSR, time, wallet labels and shares; these hashes were copied from primary explorer URLs, but the logs have not been locally decoded. **582 equal-size paired groups** were assessed by MSR: 460 had zero apparent gross price edge, 122 unfavorable, **zero favorable**. Thirteen wallets received **15.6271 PUSD** in maker-rebate transfers, too little on its own to explain the broader mechanism. This is not proof of wash trading or common control; different clients, market makers and incentive-seeking strategies remain viable.

## 5. Separate security investigation: 29 Oct Polymarket vendor-script compromise

Polymarket's [official October 30 incident report](https://status.polymarket.com/cmvvek1i90eme14ruga1z6tuj) confirms a compromised third-party monitoring script used logged-in sessions to sell positions and withdraw funds on October 29. Polymarket reported **approximately $2.7M drained and $1.2M pause-related goodwill refunds**, **$3.9M total returns**, not $3.9M stolen. It reported 95% of affected wallets fully refunded as of its October 30 update. This is an **attack on platform users**, **not** insider trading.

[Bitquery's October 29 forensic reconstruction](https://bitquery.io/investigations/polymarket-hack) reports **737 wallets drained through 985 approvals/relayer actions**, totaling **$2.73M** as measured during its investigation. The reported attacker cashout address is `0x3f99139f1c5854bb628c2e576aaf638e99dffc18` (Polygon), with splits toward `0xe4c4c39c1733e1c7c06b79f97ed442b68df41ec6`, `0x5392587814ce8c6af6789764f88e5e9ab0b9962c` and `0xc91ee0574a14446de200923729c349b9cae19b60`. Bitquery's Ethereum follow-up ends at its collection timestamp (Oct 29, 19:49 UTC). Victims, attacker identity and later exits are **not fully resolved**.

The existing [hacks-parallel](../../investigations/hacks-parallel/README.md) snapshot covers May/June 2026 and has no reported findings. This is a new, subsequent incident and should get a **new follow-up** rather than a rewrite of that older source snapshot. Source evidence must differentiate compromised browser sessions and attacker withdrawals from ordinary profitable market bets.

## 6. Midterm referrals: source context, not an identified wallet case

[NPR, October 27](https://www.wxxinews.org/npr-news/2026-10-27/polymarket-has-investigated-dozens-of-election-related-cases) reported that Polymarket investigated "dozens" of 2026 midterm trading alerts, and that at least two matters were referred to CFTC, per a source familiar with the process. No public wallet or case ID is identified for those two referrals. Neither the **October 27 Israeli election** case in our earlier external intake nor any 544-wallet ACDC cohort is shown to be one of those referrals. Treat this as **regulatory context only**, not corroboration of a named trader's guilt.
